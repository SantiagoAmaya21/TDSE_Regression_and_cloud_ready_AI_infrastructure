TA FEEDBACK TO STUDENT
------------------------------------------------------------
Good work overall. The repository is clean, all required files are
present, and the core methodology—deriving and implementing gradient
descent from first principles—is executed correctly in both notebooks.
The mandatory visualisations are all present and the conceptual
explanations demonstrate understanding of the material.

The most important issue to address is the learning rate in Notebook 2.
When features have very different scales (M ≈ 1, T ≈ 6 000,
M*T ≈ 10 000), gradient magnitudes differ by orders of magnitude and
no single fixed learning rate can work well across all of them. The
standard remedy is to standardise each feature to zero mean and unit
variance before training, then choose a learning rate in the range
0.001–0.01. Without this, your M1/M2/M3 comparison and interaction
sweep are based on an unconverged model and do not accurately reflect
the value of each feature group.

For the learning-rate experiment in Notebook 1, consider plotting all
three convergence curves on a single figure so that the speed and
stability differences are immediately visible—a print table alone does
not communicate the dynamic behaviour of the optimiser.

Finally, enrich the inference demo in Notebook 2 with a brief physical
check: a star with M = 1.3 M_sun should have L roughly between 2 and
5 L☉ according to the main-sequence power law (L ∝ M^3.5); confirming
or questioning whether your model respects this order of magnitude
adds scientific credibility to the result.
